Course Overview

Fall Semester 2026 · School of Artificial Intelligence, Tianjin University

This course is the first-semester course of the master-class project-based program in embodied intelligence. Using desktop robotic arms and the unified LeRobot toolchain as the carrier, students complete Linux/Git collaboration, hardware setup and safety, arm calibration, teleoperation, LeRobot Dataset collection, and layered reproduction and comparison of Behavior Cloning (BC), ACT, Diffusion-type policies, and SmolVLA. Centered on the "data—policy—evaluation—real-robot deployment" loop, the course builds students' engineering intuition for observations, actions, demonstration data, policy learning, and out-of-distribution generalization through runnable projects. It is the opening step of cultivating "Huo Qubing"-style embodied intelligence talent, and establishes a reproducible foundation of code, data, and evaluation for the Agentic Robotics course in the freshman spring and the deep embodied intelligence practicum in the sophomore year.

Assessment and Grading

This course adopts project-based continuous assessment and does not set a closed-book final exam as the main form of testing. The overall grade = LeRobot full-pipeline and strong-baseline reproduction (40%) + Model improvement and comparison experiments (40%) + EI paper draft and open-source materials (20%). Evidence of evaluation includes runnable code and systems, data/model/experiment logs, comparison and innovation experiments, papers or reports, cross-group reproduction, individual code walkthroughs, and oral exams. Team outcomes are assessed together with individual evidence; a passing grade requires an overall score of at least 60, plus passing the individual oral exam and the safety and integrity checks. Acceptance of an external paper is an honorary achievement and is not the sole condition for passing the course.

  • LeRobot Full-Pipeline and Strong-Baseline Reproduction (40%)
    • Complete the calibration—teleoperation—data—training—deployment loop, reproduce 2–3 representative baselines under a unified protocol, and submit environment setups, data cards, logs, and cross-group reproduction evidence.
    • 90–100: complete loop, stable results, cross-group reproducible, sufficient evidence chain with explainable errors; 60–74: complete the basic loop and at least 2 baselines, submit basic verification materials.
  • Model Improvement and Comparison Experiments (40%)
    • Propose 1 falsifiable improvement and complete main experiments, key ablations, OOD/robustness tests, and failure analysis; compare fairly with strong baselines under a unified protocol.
    • 90–100: sufficient evidence, statistically reliable, clear mechanistic explanation; 60–74: complete the main experiment and basic comparisons, with reasonably clear statements of conclusion boundaries.
  • EI Paper Draft and Open-Source Materials (20%)
    • Complete an EI-topic selection and a paper draft of at least 40%, and submit README, real-robot videos, data/model documentation, experiment records, and individual contribution statements.
    • 90–100: clear argumentation, standard figures and tables, complete materials ready to continue toward submission; 60–74: submit the required draft and basic materials, able to state one's own contributions.

The instructor and TAs will keep pre-registrations, code versions, data hashes, configurations, training logs, full real-robot episodes, failure records, demos, defenses, and contribution statements as the basis for course objective achievement and grade assignment. In cases of data fabrication, fabricated citations, serious safety violations, or inability to explain one's own core work, a one-vote veto will be applied according to the training program.

Theory and Lab Content

Theory Chapters (Lectures and Self-Study)

  • Chapter 1: Course Kickoff, Technology Landscape, and Safety: Embodied intelligence vs. traditional AI/robot control; the data—policy—evaluation life cycle; lab safety, emergency stop, and responsibility boundaries.
  • Chapter 2: Linux, Git, and Reproducible Environments: Linux file and process basics; Git branching, commits, and code review; environment management, containers, and dependency locking.
  • Chapter 3: Arm Body, Coordinate Frames, and Calibration: Joints, end effectors, and control modes; base, camera, and end-effector frames; zero-position, camera, and hand–eye calibration errors.
  • Chapter 4: Demonstration Data and Imitation Learning: Observations, states, actions, and trajectories; behavior cloning and distribution shift; data splits, leakage, and demonstration quality.
  • Chapter 5: ACT, Diffusion, and VLA Policies: Action chunking and temporal modeling; generative action distributions and sampling; vision–language–action alignment and SmolVLA.
  • Chapter 6: Experiment Design and Scientific Communication: Primary metrics, random seeds, and confidence intervals; fair data/compute budgets and pre-registration; failure classification, ablations, and the structure of an EI paper.

Lab Projects

  • Lab 1: Workstation Setup and Safety Acceptance: Equipment inventory and labeling, driver installation and communication checks, and safety checks on speed limits, joint limits, emergency stop, and workspace.
  • Lab 2: Arm and Camera Calibration: Zero-position and end-effector calibration, camera intrinsics/extrinsics and hand–eye calibration, forming a calibration dossier.
  • Lab 3: Teleoperation and Data Synchronization: Leader–follower arm or joystick teleoperation workflow, timestamp synchronization, trajectory replay, and frame-drop diagnosis.
  • Lab 4: LeRobot Dataset V0: Configure episodes and features, data splits, data cards, version numbers, and hashes, and cross-group quality audits.
  • Lab 5: Behavior Cloning Baseline: Train lightweight BC, offline evaluation, low-speed real-robot inference, and failure classification.
  • Lab 6: ACT Reproduction and Midterm Quality Gate: Compare with BC under a unified protocol, conduct cross-group reproduction, and analyze reproduction deviations.
  • Lab 7: Diffusion-Type Policy Reproduction: Train diffusion or flow-matching policies, record sampling steps, inference latency, and resource usage.
  • Lab 8: SmolVLA Inference and Small-Data Adaptation: Load pretrained models and complete inference, LoRA or small-data fine-tuning, and task adaptation.
  • Lab 9: Unified Evaluation and Innovative Experiments: Position, lighting, and object OOD tests, falsifiable improvements, key ablations, and failure mining.
  • Lab 10: Final Demo, Cross-Group Reproduction, and Paper Draft: End-to-end real-robot demos, cross-group reproduction per README, EI paper draft, and individual contribution statements.

Course Project

Project name: Comprehensive Innovative Project on Embodied Intelligence Manipulation. Based on unified data and evaluation protocols, propose a falsifiable policy or data improvement, and complete main experiments, key ablations, OOD/robustness tests, failure analysis, and the organization of reproducible materials. The project uses 6 groups × 5 students with rotating roles; instructors and TAs conduct phase reviews, code walkthroughs, cross-group reproduction, and individual defenses. The project tasks are integrated into Labs 9 and 10; code versions, data hashes, training logs, full real-robot episodes, and contribution statements must be retained.

Course Policies

Quality Gates and Passing Criteria

  • G0 Executability: Environment installable, devices communicating, data readable, and code runnable.
  • G1 Models and Project: In-class tasks completed as required, model experiments runnable, and the comprehensive project completes real-robot episodes.
  • G2 Results and Analysis: Fair evaluation conditions, complete result analysis, explainable failure causes, and documented recovery processes.
  • G3 Process and Safety: Clear configuration and model versions, complete execution logs retained, exceptions and manual takeovers recorded, and defenses and safety statements completed.
  • Passing requires an overall score of at least 60 and complete safety and process records; in grade management, pass is recorded as 100 and fail as 0.

Safety and Integrity Baselines

  • Do not bypass emergency stops, speed limits, joint limits, or workspace constraints; do not conceal major safety incidents.
  • Do not fabricate data, logs, results, or citations; do not delete failure evidence or keep only successful episodes.
  • Do not present others' code that you cannot explain as your own work; do not substitute edited videos for complete real-robot records.
  • In cases of data fabrication, fabricated citations, serious safety violations, or inability to explain one's own core work, a one-vote veto will be applied.

Final Submission Checklist

  • In-class task and data check records | System design | Environment and configuration | Model versions and checkpoints
  • Training/execution logs | Complete experiment demo records | Result analysis | Failure cases and recovery analysis
  • Exception and manual takeover records | Defense and safety statements
